# Executive Review: Back-Office Claims Automation
**Prepared for:** NexaAI Solutions Leadership Team
**Date:** January 2024
**Client:** Al-Waha Healthcare Group | **Strategic Importance:** Medium

## Summary
Delivered, but with a schedule and cost overrun (~43 days, ~31% over
budget) driven entirely by compliance boundary re-scoping rather than
technical delivery problems. ROI recovered from a weak 1.9% to a
reasonable 14.1% by close, but customer satisfaction stayed flat around
5/10 throughout, suggesting the mid-project scope changes were felt by
the client team even after resolution.

## Management Observations
This is now a second data point (alongside the Meridian chatbot
engagement) where an underestimated compliance review scope, not
technical execution, was the primary driver of overrun — though the
specific pattern here is distinct: Automation-First strategy applied to
a healthcare account, where the operations-vs-compliance definition of
"routine" diverged mid-project.

## Strategic Implications
Recommend that any future Automation-First proposal for a healthcare or
clinical-adjacent client require a compliance-officer-level review of
each proposed automation category at proposal stage, not just an
operations-team review — this is now a documented, recurring gap
specific to this industry vertical, distinct from Automation-First's
generally strong track record in Manufacturing and Logistics.

## Open Items
Al-Waha has not yet indicated interest in expanding automation scope;
recommend revisiting after the current escalation-queue pattern has run
long enough to build a track record with their Compliance function.
