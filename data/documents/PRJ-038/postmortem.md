# Post-Mortem: Defect Detection Computer Vision Pilot (Cancelled)
**Date:** April 2023
**Status at close:** Cancelled roughly 7 weeks into a planned 12-week
pilot; actual cost $49.9K against a $159.1K budget (partial spend); no
revenue recognized

## What Happened
The pilot was cancelled after line quality engineers, who had not been
consulted before the pilot was approved at the Plant Manager level,
quietly deprioritized cooperation with data collection — root-caused to
quality engineers perceiving the pilot as an implicit challenge to
expertise they had personally developed over years, rather than a
complement to it.

## Root Cause
This is a sponsorship-level gap distinct from the Horizon Auto
Components procurement cancellation (PRJ-017), though both ultimately
manifest as an organizational-resistance/adoption failure: there, the
gap was leadership's description of the process not matching front-line
practice; here, the gap was that front-line staff whose expertise was
directly adjacent to the proposed automation were never brought into the
conversation before the pilot was approved and scoped.

## What Could Have Gone Better
Proposal-stage sponsorship sat entirely with the Plant Manager. For any
pilot proposing to automate a task that a specific technical role has
built deep, personally-invested expertise in (manual visual inspection,
here), that role's practitioners should be consulted — not just informed
— before the pilot is scoped, regardless of who the formal sponsor is.

## Lessons Learned
"Who sponsors the pilot" and "whose expertise does the pilot's subject
matter touch" are not always the same stakeholder group, and treating
sponsorship-level buy-in as sufficient when they diverge is a specific,
recurring risk pattern — now documented on two separate cancelled
engagements (this one and PRJ-017) with two distinct specific causes
under the same general "organizational resistance" category.
